Norlake Programmable Controller (Carel PCO5+ based walk-in control panel) · DOOR AJAR ALARM

Why is my Norlake walk-in showing DOOR AJAR ALARM?

Short answer: Door open too long. If your Norlake Programmable Controller (Carel PCO5+ based walk-in control panel) is showing DOOR AJAR ALARM, the controller has logged a specific, documented condition. This page covers what it means, what causes it, what you can safely fix yourself, and when — and why — it takes a technician.

What exactly is the machine telling you?

The door digital input has been active longer than the Door Al Delay setting, indicating the walk-in door has been left open or the door switch has failed.

Which machines show this?

This is Norlake's programmable walk-in controller — a control panel built on the Carel PCO5+ platform, supplied with Norlake walk-in coolers and freezers and their refrigeration packages. Unlike the tiny code-flashing thermostats on many boxes, this one has a multi-line LCD that spells alarms out in full English — DOOR AJAR ALARM, ROOM TEMP HIGH ALARM, AIR FLOW LOSS — with an ALARM button that silences the buzzer and steps through active alarms. The status line on MENU 1 also tells you when the controller is running on a backup sensor. Practical tip: pressing ALARM after closing a left-open door doesn't just silence the noise — it deliberately delays the air-temperature alarm to give the room time to recover, which is exactly what Norlake intends you to do.

What causes it?

  • The walk-in door left open longer than the Door Al Delay setting
  • A damaged gasket, closer, or latch — or ice on them — keeping the door from sealing
  • A door switch failed, stuck, or out of alignment

Can you fix it yourself?

Usually. Close the door fully, press ALARM to silence the buzzer, and check gasket, closer, latch, and switch alignment — that covers the common cases.

Step by step — what to try, in order:

  1. Close the door fully and press the ALARM button to silence the buzzer
  2. Check the door gasket, closer, and latch for damage or ice
  3. Verify the door switch is aligned and not stuck
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.

When do you need a technician — and why?

If the alarm persists with the door genuinely closed, the switch or its wiring needs continuity testing — a short technician task, but meter work on the control circuit.

When to call: Call a tech if the alarm persists with the door closed — the switch or its wiring needs testing.
