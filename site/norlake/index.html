Norlake · 22 codes and messages

What do Norlake programmable walk-in controller alarms mean?

Short answer: Norlake's programmable panel spells alarms out in plain English. The ALARM button glows red and a buzzer sounds; press ALARM once to silence the buzzer, press it again to see the first alarm screen, and keep pressing to step through each active alarm until "NO MORE ALARMS". Each screen shows the date and time, and some carry a hint — for example "AIR FLOW LOSS 1 — Check Blower, Belts".

Which machines use it?

This is Norlake's programmable walk-in controller — a control panel built on the Carel PCO5+ platform, supplied with Norlake walk-in coolers and freezers and their refrigeration packages. Unlike the tiny code-flashing thermostats on many boxes, this one has a multi-line LCD that spells alarms out in full English — DOOR AJAR ALARM, ROOM TEMP HIGH ALARM, AIR FLOW LOSS — with an ALARM button that silences the buzzer and steps through active alarms. The status line on MENU 1 also tells you when the controller is running on a backup sensor. Practical tip: pressing ALARM after closing a left-open door doesn't just silence the noise — it deliberately delays the air-temperature alarm to give the room time to recover, which is exactly what Norlake intends you to do.

How do you read and clear the display?

Read the status line on MENU 1 too: USING PROD SENSOR means the air temperature sensor has failed and the room is being held on the product sensor; SENSOR FAIL MODE means the air and product sensors have all failed and — if sensor-fail control is enabled — the unit is running on the fixed Cool On / Heat On times. Cooling continues in both cases, but on backup logic.

What does each code mean — and what should you do?

DOOR AJAR ALARM — Door open too long

The door digital input has been active longer than the Door Al Delay setting, indicating the walk-in door has been left open or the door switch has failed.

What causes it:

  • The walk-in door left open longer than the Door Al Delay setting
  • A damaged gasket, closer, or latch — or ice on them — keeping the door from sealing
  • A door switch failed, stuck, or out of alignment

Can staff fix it? Usually. Close the door fully, press ALARM to silence the buzzer, and check gasket, closer, latch, and switch alignment — that covers the common cases.

What to check, in order:

  1. Close the door fully and press the ALARM button to silence the buzzer
  2. Check the door gasket, closer, and latch for damage or ice
  3. Verify the door switch is aligned and not stuck

Why it takes a technician: If the alarm persists with the door genuinely closed, the switch or its wiring needs continuity testing — a short technician task, but meter work on the control circuit.

When to call: Call a tech if the alarm persists with the door closed — the switch or its wiring needs testing.

Source: Norlake — Programmable Controller — operation manual (113470 Rev. J, 6/20)

ROOM TEMP HIGH ALARM — Room air temperature above high limit

Room air temperature exceeded the user-set high alarm limit for longer than the alarm delay. Norlake notes this is an early warning that often occurs when the door is left open too long; closing the door and pressing ALARM delays the air alarm while temperature recovers.

What causes it:

  • The door left open too long — Norlake flags this as the most common trigger for this early warning
  • Ice on the evaporator coil or fans not running
  • Condensing unit off or its coil airflow blocked
  • A tripped breaker on the refrigeration circuit

Can staff fix it? Often. Close the door and press ALARM — that silences and deliberately delays the alarm while temperature recovers — then verify coil, fans, condensing unit, and breaker. Most of these resolve without a truck roll.

What to check, in order:

  1. Close the door and press the ALARM button to silence and delay the alarm
  2. Check the evaporator coil for ice and confirm the fans are running
  3. Confirm the condensing unit is running and its coil airflow is clear
  4. Check the breaker for the refrigeration circuit

Why it takes a technician: If the room doesn't come back to range after the door is closed and airflow verified, the remaining causes are refrigerant-side — sealed-system diagnosis under EPA Section 608 — and warm product doesn't wait.

When to call: Call a refrigeration tech if temperature does not return to range after the door has been closed and airflow verified.

Source: Norlake — Programmable Controller — operation manual (113470 Rev. J, 6/20)

ROOM TEMP LOW ALARM — Room air temperature below low limit

Room air temperature dropped below the user-set low alarm limit for longer than the alarm delay, indicating the room is overcooling.

What causes it:

  • Setpoints changed on the SET screens
  • A stuck cooling relay or contactor keeping refrigeration running past setpoint
  • Freeze-sensitive product sitting in the evaporator discharge air

Can staff fix it? Partially. Verify the real temperature with a separate thermometer, check the SET screens for changed setpoints, and move freeze-sensitive product out of the discharge air.

What to check, in order:

  1. Verify the actual room temperature with a separate thermometer
  2. Check whether setpoints were recently changed on the SET screens
  3. Move freeze-sensitive product away from the evaporator discharge

Why it takes a technician: A room that keeps running below setpoint points at a stuck relay or contactor — live electrical diagnosis in the control panel — and in a cooler, sustained overcooling freezes product.

When to call: Call a tech if the room keeps running below setpoint — a stuck cooling relay or contactor needs professional diagnosis.

Source: Norlake — Programmable Controller — operation manual (113470 Rev. J, 6/20)

ROOM TEMPERATURE SENSOR FAILURE — Air temperature sensor failed

The room air sensor has failed. The status line will show "USING PROD SENSOR" if the controller falls back to the product sensor, or "SENSOR FAIL MODE" if all sensors have failed and the unit runs on timed Cool On/Heat On cycles.

What causes it:

  • The room air sensor has failed outright
  • The controller running on fallback — "USING PROD SENSOR" if the product sensor took over, or "SENSOR FAIL MODE" timed cycling if every sensor is gone

Can staff fix it? No — but staff should check the MENU 1 status line to see which fallback the controller is in, verify box temperature with a separate thermometer, and record both for the tech. Fallback keeps the room alive, not well-controlled.

What to check, in order:

  1. Check the MENU 1 status line to see which fallback mode the controller is in
  2. Verify box temperature with a separate thermometer
  3. Record the alarm and status for the service tech

Why it takes a technician: The room is running on backup control, which holds temperature roughly at best — timed cycling can drift either direction. The sensor needs testing and replacement promptly, before backup control turns into a product problem.

When to call: Call a tech promptly — the room is running on backup control and needs the sensor tested and replaced.

Source: Norlake — Programmable Controller — operation manual (113470 Rev. J, 6/20)

AIR FLOW LOSS 1 — Evaporator air flow lost

The air flow switch for circuit 1 has tripped, indicating the evaporator blower is not moving air. The alarm screen itself advises checking the blower and belts. (AIR FLOW LOSS 2 is the same alarm for the second circuit.)

What causes it:

  • The evaporator blower for circuit 1 not moving air — the air flow switch tripped
  • A tripped breaker on the evaporator fan circuit
  • Ice physically blocking the fan blades or coil
  • A failed blower motor or belt — the alarm screen itself says check blower and belts

Can staff fix it? Partially. Look at the evaporator — are the blades turning? Check the fan-circuit breaker and look for ice jamming the blades. A tripped breaker or visible ice block is staff-resolvable; a dead motor is not.

What to check, in order:

  1. Look at the evaporator: are the fan blades turning?
  2. Check the breaker for the evaporator fan circuit
  3. Check for ice blocking the fan blades or coil

Why it takes a technician: If the fans won't run with power confirmed on, motor, belt, or contactor replacement is a service job. With no air moving across the coil, the room stops cooling regardless of how well the refrigeration side works.

When to call: Call a tech if fans are not running with power on — motor, belt, or contactor replacement is a service job.

Source: Norlake — Programmable Controller — operation manual (113470 Rev. J, 6/20)

POWER RESTART — Power was interrupted

If the Power Reset Alarm is enabled, this alarm records that controller power was interrupted; it appears about 10 seconds after power-up. It does not affect control of the room — it only logs the outage.

What causes it:

  • Controller power was interrupted and the Power Reset Alarm feature logged it — it appears about 10 seconds after power-up
  • A building outage, or something more local like a failing breaker or wiring issue if it recurs without a known outage

Can staff fix it? Yes — this one is informational. Press ALARM to view and acknowledge it, confirm room and product temperatures recovered, and check whether other equipment on the circuit also blinked.

What to check, in order:

  1. Press the ALARM button to view and acknowledge the alarm
  2. Verify room and product temperatures recovered after the outage
  3. Check whether other equipment on the same circuit also lost power

Why it takes a technician: The alarm never affects control of the room. The only reason to bring in an electrician or tech is repeated power interruptions with no known outage — that pattern points at a failing breaker or wiring fault worth catching early.

When to call: Call an electrician or tech if power interruptions recur without a known outage — that can indicate a failing breaker or wiring issue.

Source: Norlake — Programmable Controller — operation manual (113470 Rev. J, 6/20)

How do you diagnose a Norlake programmable controller alarm, step by step?

  1. Silence, then read every screen. ALARM once for the buzzer, then step through all active alarms — more than one may be active.
  2. Check the status line for USING PROD SENSOR or SENSOR FAIL MODE (above).
  3. Most alarms come from digital inputs. Compressor 1 and 2 alarms, air flow 1 and 2, door, water, smoke, humidity and drain are all inputs, each configurable as normally open or normally closed; the alarm sounds when the input matches its "Active if" setting for longer than the delay. A wrongly configured or failed input device can raise an alarm even when the refrigeration is fine.
  4. AIR FLOW LOSS: look at the blower first. The alarm screen itself says Check Blower, Belts.
  5. Sensor failures: the standard air sensor is a 10k thermistor (10,000 Ω; a 1k RTD is optional), and the board's NTC inputs are 10 kΩ ±1 % at 25 °C. Sensor calibration offsets are under the AUX2 key — a sensor that reads slightly off can be offset, but a failed one must be replaced.
  6. POWER RESTART is informational. If enabled it appears 10 seconds after power-up, and the room keeps running on its configuration. The separate power-failure alarm uses a battery switch to the right of the user terminal: top = test, centre = off, bottom = on.
  7. Ring Back sets how many minutes the buzzer stays silent after an alarm is silenced — if it keeps coming back, the condition is still there.
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.
