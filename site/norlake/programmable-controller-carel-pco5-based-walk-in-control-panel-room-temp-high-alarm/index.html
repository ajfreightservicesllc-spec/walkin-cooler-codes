Norlake Programmable Controller (Carel PCO5+ based walk-in control panel) · ROOM TEMP HIGH ALARM

Why is my Norlake walk-in showing ROOM TEMP HIGH ALARM?

Short answer: Room air temperature above high limit. If your Norlake Programmable Controller (Carel PCO5+ based walk-in control panel) is showing ROOM TEMP HIGH ALARM, the controller has logged a specific, documented condition. This page covers what it means, what causes it, what you can safely fix yourself, and when — and why — it takes a technician.

What exactly is the machine telling you?

Room air temperature exceeded the user-set high alarm limit for longer than the alarm delay. Norlake notes this is an early warning that often occurs when the door is left open too long; closing the door and pressing ALARM delays the air alarm while temperature recovers.

Which machines show this?

This is Norlake's programmable walk-in controller — a control panel built on the Carel PCO5+ platform, supplied with Norlake walk-in coolers and freezers and their refrigeration packages. Unlike the tiny code-flashing thermostats on many boxes, this one has a multi-line LCD that spells alarms out in full English — DOOR AJAR ALARM, ROOM TEMP HIGH ALARM, AIR FLOW LOSS — with an ALARM button that silences the buzzer and steps through active alarms. The status line on MENU 1 also tells you when the controller is running on a backup sensor. Practical tip: pressing ALARM after closing a left-open door doesn't just silence the noise — it deliberately delays the air-temperature alarm to give the room time to recover, which is exactly what Norlake intends you to do.

What causes it?

  • The door left open too long — Norlake flags this as the most common trigger for this early warning
  • Ice on the evaporator coil or fans not running
  • Condensing unit off or its coil airflow blocked
  • A tripped breaker on the refrigeration circuit

Can you fix it yourself?

Often. Close the door and press ALARM — that silences and deliberately delays the alarm while temperature recovers — then verify coil, fans, condensing unit, and breaker. Most of these resolve without a truck roll.

Step by step — what to try, in order:

  1. Close the door and press the ALARM button to silence and delay the alarm
  2. Check the evaporator coil for ice and confirm the fans are running
  3. Confirm the condensing unit is running and its coil airflow is clear
  4. Check the breaker for the refrigeration circuit
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.

When do you need a technician — and why?

If the room doesn't come back to range after the door is closed and airflow verified, the remaining causes are refrigerant-side — sealed-system diagnosis under EPA Section 608 — and warm product doesn't wait.

When to call: Call a refrigeration tech if temperature does not return to range after the door has been closed and airflow verified.
