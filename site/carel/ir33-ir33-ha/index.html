Carel ir33 / ir33+ · HA

What does HA mean on a Carel ir33 / ir33+ walk-in cooler?

Short answer: HACCP alarm type HA (HF = power-failure type). If your Carel ir33 / ir33+ is showing HA, the controller has logged a specific, documented condition. This page covers what it means, what causes it, what you can safely fix yourself, and when — and why — it takes a technician.

What exactly is the machine telling you?

A HACCP temperature-excursion event was recorded: HA logs time above the high-temperature threshold during normal operation, HF logs a high-temperature condition following a power failure. These are food-safety log alarms, not equipment faults by themselves.

Which machines show this?

The Carel ir33 and ir33+ are among the most widely used electronic refrigeration controllers in the world, fitted by walk-in box and case OEMs and installed by contractors as replacement controls on coolers and freezers of every brand. The display normally shows temperature; a fault alternates a short code with the reading — E-codes for probe failures, HI/LO for temperature alarms, dor for the door, cht/CHt for the condenser, HA/HF for HACCP excursion logs. Practical tip: lowercase and uppercase matter — cht is an early dirty-condenser warning you can act on with a coil cleaning, while CHt is the escalated manual-reset alarm that means the warning stage was missed.

What causes it?

  • A HACCP temperature excursion was recorded: HA logs time above the high-temperature threshold in normal operation, HF logs a high-temperature condition after a power failure
  • Behind it, the usual HI drivers: a door or loading event, a tripped breaker or outage, or blocked condenser airflow
  • These are food-safety log alarms — not equipment faults by themselves

Can you fix it yourself?

The response is staff work: check current box temperature and whether product is still safe, identify the outage or door event, record everything per your food-safety procedure before resetting, then fix the underlying cause as you would a HI alarm.

Step by step — what to try, in order:

  1. Check current box temperature and whether product is still in safe range
  2. Check for a recent power outage (HF) or a door/loading event (HA)
  3. Record the event per your food-safety procedure before resetting
  4. Address the underlying cause (door, breaker, condenser airflow) as with a HI alarm
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.
Safety: Food safety event — document per HACCP procedure.

When do you need a technician — and why?

Repeated excursions with no obvious cause mean the equipment needs professional diagnosis — and product disposition after an excursion is a call for your food-safety manager, documented for HACCP records.

When to call: Call a tech if excursions repeat without an obvious cause — and involve your food-safety manager for product disposition.
