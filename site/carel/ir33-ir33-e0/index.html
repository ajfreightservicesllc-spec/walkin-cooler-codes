Carel ir33 / ir33+ · E0

What does E0 mean on a Carel ir33 / ir33+ walk-in cooler?

Short answer: Room probe S1 fault. If your Carel ir33 / ir33+ is showing E0, the controller has logged a specific, documented condition. This page covers what it means, what causes it, what you can safely fix yourself, and when — and why — it takes a technician.

What exactly is the machine telling you?

The room (air) temperature probe S1 is faulty, disconnected, or out of range. Depending on setup the controller may fall back to timed compressor cycling until the probe is fixed.

Which machines show this?

The Carel ir33 and ir33+ are among the most widely used electronic refrigeration controllers in the world, fitted by walk-in box and case OEMs and installed by contractors as replacement controls on coolers and freezers of every brand. The display normally shows temperature; a fault alternates a short code with the reading — E-codes for probe failures, HI/LO for temperature alarms, dor for the door, cht/CHt for the condenser, HA/HF for HACCP excursion logs. Practical tip: lowercase and uppercase matter — cht is an early dirty-condenser warning you can act on with a coil cleaning, while CHt is the escalated manual-reset alarm that means the warning stage was missed.

What causes it?

  • The S1 room (air) probe is faulty, disconnected, or reading out of range
  • Ice built up around the probe, or the probe knocked loose by product or a pallet
  • Damaged probe wiring

Can you fix it yourself?

The visual checks are: clear ice from around the probe, make sure it hasn't been hit, inspect the visible wire, power-cycle once. Depending on setup the controller may limp along on timed compressor cycling, but that's a stopgap, not a fix.

Step by step — what to try, in order:

  1. Check whether the temperature display still makes sense or shows dashes
  2. Look at the air probe inside the box — clear any ice buildup around it and make sure it hasn't been hit by product or a pallet
  3. Visually inspect the visible probe wire for damage
  4. Power-cycle at the breaker once and see if E0 clears
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.

When do you need a technician — and why?

A failed room probe means the box is running blind and product is at risk — probe testing and replacement is live electrical work at the controller that shouldn't wait.

When to call: Call a tech if E0 stays on — a failed probe means the box is running blind and product is at risk.
