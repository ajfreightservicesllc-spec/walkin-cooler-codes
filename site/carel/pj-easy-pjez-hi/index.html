Carel PJ Easy (PJEZ) · HI

What does HI mean on a Carel PJ Easy walk-in cooler?

Short answer: High temperature alarm. If your Carel PJ Easy (PJEZ) is showing HI, the controller has logged a specific, documented condition. This page covers what it means, what causes it, what you can safely fix yourself, and when — and why — it takes a technician.

What exactly is the machine telling you?

Temperature has exceeded the high alarm threshold (AH) for longer than the delay (Ad). The most common walk-in alarm; often door- or defrost-related rather than a mechanical failure.

Which machines show this?

The Carel PJ Easy (PJEZ) is a small, low-cost panel-mount controller that box and case OEMs fit widely to coolers, freezers, and walk-in panels, and that contractors commonly install as a replacement thermostat. It uses short two- or three-character codes that alternate with the temperature reading: E0/E1/E2 for failed probes, HI/LO for temperature alarms, dOR (shown as 'dr' on some models) for the door, cht/CHt for a dirty condenser, EE/EF for parameter memory errors. Practical tip: dF flashing on the display just means a defrost is running — it's normal status, not a fault, so wait out the cycle before assuming something is wrong.

What causes it?

  • Door open or gasket not sealing — the most common walk-in alarm is usually door- or defrost-related, not mechanical
  • Warm product loaded or heavy door traffic — recovery takes time
  • A defrost that just finished
  • A dust-blocked condenser coil, dead condenser fan, or tripped breaker

Can you fix it yourself?

Often, yes. Door, gasket, loading, post-defrost recovery, condenser airflow, and the breaker are all staff checks — give the box a couple of hours to recover with the door shut.

Step by step — what to try, in order:

  1. Check the door is closed and the gasket seals; look for product blocking it
  2. Consider recent loading of warm product or heavy door traffic — allow recovery time
  3. Check whether a defrost just finished
  4. Check the condensing unit: fan running, coil clear of dust and debris, breaker on
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.

When do you need a technician — and why?

If temperature keeps rising or won't recover in that window, the fault is in the sealed system — gauge diagnosis requiring EPA Section 608 certification, with product warming the whole time.

When to call: Call a tech if temperature keeps rising or doesn't recover within a couple of hours with the door shut.
