Walk-In Cooler Repair & Service in Nashville, TN

Who actually fixes commercial ice machines in the Nashville area — plus what to check before you pay for a service call.

A walk-in cooler failing overnight is one of the most expensive small disasters in the restaurant business — and Nashville, with one of the fastest-growing restaurant and hotel markets in the Southeast, has thousands of walk-in boxes working around the clock, from Broadway kitchens to hotel banquet operations to the grocery and healthcare coolers that never get a night off. When one of them starts alarming, what's beeping is almost never the box itself: it's the refrigeration system and its controller — most commonly Heatcraft equipment (sold under the Bohn and Larkin names), a KE2 Therm controller, or a Dixell controller on boxes from manufacturers like Norlake and Kolpak.

That matters because the alarm code on that little controller tells you a lot before anyone rolls a truck. A door-switch alarm or a high-temperature alarm after a delivery is a two-minute fix; a low-superheat error repeating every hour is a compressor-killer in progress. The code pages on this site translate each one.

Frequently asked questions

Who repairs walk-in coolers and freezers in Nashville?

Nashville has dedicated commercial refrigeration contractors ranging from family shops that focus specifically on walk-ins to large CFESA-certified kitchen equipment companies with 24/7 dispatch. Most service all the common systems — Heatcraft/Bohn/Larkin refrigeration, and boxes from Norlake, Kolpak, and others — regardless of who installed them.

My walk-in is warm — is it an emergency?

Treat it as one until you know otherwise, because product is on the clock. First checks: is the door sealing (gasket, latch, nothing propping it open)? Is the evaporator coil inside iced over? Is the condensing unit running with a clean coil and clear airflow? Is the breaker on? If a controller code is displayed, look it up on this site — some alarms auto-clear as temperature recovers, others mean the system has shut itself down and every hour costs you inventory.

How much product time do I have if the cooler is down?

A closed, full walk-in holds temperature far longer than an empty one — keep the door shut and don't keep checking it. Food safety rules put strict limits on time above 41°F for a cooler, so start monitoring temperature immediately, log it for your health inspector, and line up backup cold storage if the tech can't come fast. Never guess: a $30 thermometer log can save a full inventory writeoff argument with your insurer.

What maintenance actually prevents walk-in breakdowns?

Three things cover most failures: keep the condenser coil clean (quarterly in a greasy kitchen), keep the door hardware honest (gaskets, sweeps, closers, and the door switch), and don't ignore icing on the evaporator — ice on the coil always has a cause, usually a defrost problem or a door leak, and it gets more expensive the longer it runs.
