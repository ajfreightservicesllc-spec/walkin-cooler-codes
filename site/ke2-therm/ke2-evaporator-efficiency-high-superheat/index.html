KE2 Therm KE2 Evaporator Efficiency · HIGH SUPERHEAT

Why does my KE2 controller say HIGH SUPERHEAT?

Short answer: High superheat (EEV systems only). If your KE2 Therm KE2 Evaporator Efficiency is showing HIGH SUPERHEAT, the controller has logged a specific, documented condition. This page covers what it means, what causes it, what you can safely fix yourself, and when — and why — it takes a technician.

What exactly is the machine telling you?

Only on systems with an electric expansion valve: superheat has run 2°F above setpoint for 90 minutes with the valve at least 95% open. Commonly indicates low refrigerant charge or a valve problem. Yellow LED lit; system keeps running.

Which machines show this?

The KE2 Evaporator Efficiency (KE2 Evap) from KE2 Therm is an aftermarket and OEM evaporator controller found on walk-in coolers and freezers of nearly any box brand — it's one of the most common retrofit controls in the country, often installed to add demand defrost and remote monitoring to an existing walk-in. If your evaporator has a small KE2 display that scrolls text alarms in plain words (HIGH AIR TEMP, DOOR SWITCH, EXCESS DEFROST) rather than cryptic codes, this is it. A yellow LED means a non-critical alarm with the system still running; a red LED is critical. Practical tip: the VARIABLES menu shows live sensor readings — a temperature of -87 means an unplugged sensor, 180+ means a shorted one.

What causes it?

  • Low refrigerant charge — the common culprit when the valve has been ≥95% open for 90 minutes and superheat still runs high
  • An electric expansion valve problem
  • A dirty or airflow-blocked condensing unit coil making the system work short-handed

Can you fix it yourself?

Barely. Clearing debris off the condensing unit coil is the one useful staff move. Everything else on this alarm is refrigerant-side — note the alarm, room temperature, and duration, and make the call.

Step by step — what to try, in order:

  1. Note the alarm, room temperature, and how long it has been active
  2. Check the condensing unit coil for dirt or blocked airflow and clear any debris
  3. Do not attempt refrigerant-side checks
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.

When do you need a technician — and why?

Checking suction pressure, refrigerant charge, and valve operation takes gauges on the sealed system, which is EPA Section 608 certified work by law. Low charge also usually means a leak that must be found, not just topped off.

When to call: Call a refrigeration tech promptly — diagnosing suction pressure, refrigerant charge, and valve operation requires gauges and EPA certification.
