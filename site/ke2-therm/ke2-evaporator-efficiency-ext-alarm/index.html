KE2 Therm KE2 Evaporator Efficiency · EXT ALARM

Why does my KE2 controller say EXT ALARM?

Short answer: External device alarm passed through. If your KE2 Therm KE2 Evaporator Efficiency is showing EXT ALARM, the controller has logged a specific, documented condition. This page covers what it means, what causes it, what you can safely fix yourself, and when — and why — it takes a technician.

What exactly is the machine telling you?

A third-party device wired to a digital input (for example a condensate pan overflow float switch) has tripped. The controller displays and forwards the alarm but refrigeration control continues normally.

Which machines show this?

The KE2 Evaporator Efficiency (KE2 Evap) from KE2 Therm is an aftermarket and OEM evaporator controller found on walk-in coolers and freezers of nearly any box brand — it's one of the most common retrofit controls in the country, often installed to add demand defrost and remote monitoring to an existing walk-in. If your evaporator has a small KE2 display that scrolls text alarms in plain words (HIGH AIR TEMP, DOOR SWITCH, EXCESS DEFROST) rather than cryptic codes, this is it. A yellow LED means a non-critical alarm with the system still running; a red LED is critical. Practical tip: the VARIABLES menu shows live sensor readings — a temperature of -87 means an unplugged sensor, 180+ means a shorted one.

What causes it?

  • A third-party device wired to the digital input has tripped — most commonly a condensate pan overflow float switch
  • A clogged drain line backing water up into the pan until the float lifts
  • Refrigeration control itself is unaffected; the controller is only passing the message along

Can you fix it yourself?

Frequently. If a drain-pan float is the connected device, checking the pan for standing water and clearing a visible blockage at the drain outlet is well within staff ability, and cooling never stopped.

Step by step — what to try, in order:

  1. Identify which external device is wired to the controller (often a drain pan float switch)
  2. Check the drain pan for standing water or a clogged drain line if a float switch is installed
  3. Clear any visible blockage at the drain outlet
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.

When do you need a technician — and why?

If the device keeps tripping, or nobody on site knows what is actually wired to that input, a technician needs to trace the input and fix the underlying device — repeated pan overflows, for example, mean a drain problem that will eventually leak into the box.

When to call: Call a tech if the external device keeps tripping or you can't identify what is connected to the alarm input.
