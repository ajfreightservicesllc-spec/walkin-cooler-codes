Dixell (Emerson) Fault & Error Codes

12 documented codes and fault signals, verified against service manuals, with plain-English fixes.

The small Dixell XR-series controller — now an Emerson/Copeland product — is probably the most common digital thermostat on walk-in coolers in America. Box builders including Norlake and Kolpak have shipped it for years, so the two-letter codes on that little display are the fault language of a huge share of walk-ins. The vocabulary is short: P1 through P4 are probe (sensor) failures, HA and LA are high and low temperature alarms, HA2/LA2 condenser temperature alarms, dA the door alarm, EA an external device alarm — and CA is the serious one, meaning a critical input tripped and the controller switched every output off.

The most useful thing to know about a Dixell is what it does during a probe failure: on P1 the controller keeps the box running on timed compressor cycles instead of actual temperature, so the cooler limps along while quietly needing a sensor. And the code that generates the most confused service calls isn't a fault at all — OF or PoF just means the keypad is locked (hold the up and down arrows for three seconds).
