Danfoss Optyma Plus condensing unit controller · A96

What does A96 mean on a Danfoss Optyma Plus condensing unit controller?

Short answer: Maximum discharge temperature exceeded. If your Danfoss Optyma Plus condensing unit controller is showing A96, the controller has logged a specific, documented condition. This page covers what it means, what causes it, what you can safely fix yourself, and when — and why — it takes a technician.

What exactly is the machine telling you?

The compressor discharge gas temperature has exceeded its limit and the controller protects the compressor. Often related to condenser airflow or refrigerant-side problems.

Which machines show this?

Danfoss Optyma Plus condensing units — the outdoor or machine-room half of many walk-in cooler and freezer installations — carry their own built-in electronic controller with a small display on the unit. It reports faults as short codes: A-codes for alarms (A2 low suction pressure, A17 pressure safety trip, A80 condenser airflow, A96 discharge temperature) and E-codes for controller defects. Because this controller lives on the condensing unit, its alarms lean toward compressor protection — pressure trips and overheating — rather than box temperature. Practical tip: when one of these codes stops the compressor, resist the urge to keep resetting; the protections exist because forcing restarts is how compressors get destroyed.

What causes it?

  • Compressor discharge gas temperature over its limit — the controller steps in to protect the compressor
  • Blocked or dirty condenser airflow
  • The unit enclosed in a hot, unventilated space
  • Refrigerant-side problems driving discharge temperature up

Can you fix it yourself?

Only the airflow checks: clean visible dirt off the condenser (power off first) and make sure the unit isn't boxed into a hot corner. Avoid repeated resets.

Step by step — what to try, in order:

  1. Check the condenser for blocked airflow and clean visible dirt (power off first)
  2. Make sure the unit isn't enclosed in a hot, unventilated space
  3. Monitor box temperature and avoid repeated resets
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.
Safety: Refrigerant-side issue — do not force restarts.

When do you need a technician — and why?

Overheating discharge gas destroys compressors if the root cause isn't fixed — the refrigerant-side diagnosis behind it is gauge work for an EPA Section 608-certified tech, not something to reset past.

When to call: Call a tech if the alarm returns — overheating discharge gas will destroy the compressor if the root cause isn't fixed.
