Danfoss AK-CC 210/250 · A16

What does A16 mean on a Danfoss AK-CC 210/250 walk-in cooler?

Short answer: Alarm signal from DI2 input. If your Danfoss AK-CC 210/250 is showing A16, the controller has logged a specific, documented condition. This page covers what it means, what causes it, what you can safely fix yourself, and when — and why — it takes a technician.

What exactly is the machine telling you?

An external device wired to the DI2 digital input is signaling an alarm after the configured delay. What tripped depends on what is connected to DI2.

Which machines show this?

The Danfoss AK-CC 210 and AK-CC 250 are case controllers for commercial refrigeration — cold rooms, display cases, and walk-in evaporator sections, frequently networked into a central monitoring system that logs their alarms. They report with the same short-code convention as the rest of Danfoss's EKC/AK line: E-codes for sensor and hardware defects (E25 and E26 are the S3 and S4 air sensors) and A-codes for alarm conditions, including A60, a dedicated HACCP high-temperature alarm for food-safety monitoring. Practical tip: when A60 shows, log the actual product temperature immediately — that alarm is a compliance record as much as an equipment warning, and your notes matter at inspection time.

What causes it?

  • An external device wired to the DI2 digital input tripped and held past the configured delay
  • Often a protective switch that opened for a reason — what it means depends on what the installer wired to DI2

Can you fix it yourself?

No. Check the box is still cooling, note any other alarms on nearby equipment, and record the code and time — that's real help for the tech, not a fix.

Step by step — what to try, in order:

  1. Check box temperature and whether the unit still cools
  2. Note any other alarms on nearby equipment
  3. Record the code and time for the tech
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.

When do you need a technician — and why?

Only someone who can trace the installation can identify the device on DI2, and if it's a pressure or safety switch, the root cause behind the trip needs professional diagnosis before the unit is trusted again.

When to call: Call your tech to identify the device on DI2 — it is often a protective switch that tripped for a reason.
