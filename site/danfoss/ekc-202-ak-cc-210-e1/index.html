Danfoss EKC 202 / AK-CC 210 · E1

What does E1 mean on a Danfoss EKC 202 / AK-CC 210 walk-in cooler?

Short answer: Controller fault. If your Danfoss EKC 202 / AK-CC 210 is showing E1, the controller has logged a specific, documented condition. This page covers what it means, what causes it, what you can safely fix yourself, and when — and why — it takes a technician.

What exactly is the machine telling you?

Internal fault in the controller itself ('EKC error'). This is a hardware/electronics problem, not a temperature condition.

Which machines show this?

The Danfoss EKC 202 and its successor the AK-CC 210 are panel-mount case controllers used across commercial refrigeration — display cases, cold rooms, and walk-in evaporator control, often tied into a store's front-end monitoring network. Faults show as short codes plus a plain-text line on the display: E-codes are defects (E1 controller fault, E27 and E29 sensor errors) and A-codes are alarms (A1 high temperature, A2 low, A4 door, A45 standby). Practical tip: A45 standby doesn't trip the alarm relay on the AK-CC 210 — a box someone left in standby can sit warming quietly, so treat an unexplained 'Standby mode' display as urgent, not informational.

What causes it?

  • An internal fault in the controller's own electronics — the 'EKC error'
  • Not a temperature condition: the hardware itself is the problem

Can you fix it yourself?

One shot: power-cycle at the breaker for a few minutes. If E1 returns, there's nothing else staff can do except record it and watch box temperature with a separate thermometer.

Step by step — what to try, in order:

  1. Power-cycle the unit at the breaker for 2-3 minutes
  2. If the code returns, record it and monitor box temperature with a separate thermometer
  3. Move product if the unit is not holding temperature
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.

When do you need a technician — and why?

A persistent internal fault means the controller likely needs replacement — swapping and reprogramming a refrigeration controller is live electrical work, and until then the box's control can't be trusted.

When to call: If E1 persists after a power cycle, the controller likely needs replacement by a tech.
