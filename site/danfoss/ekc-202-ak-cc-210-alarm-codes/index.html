Danfoss · 30 codes and messages

What do Danfoss EKC 202 and AK-CC 210 alarm codes mean?

Short answer: these controllers show three kinds of code. E-codes are faults — a sensor error or a defect in the controller. A-codes are alarms from daily operation — temperature, door, inputs, standby. S-codes are status messages that explain why nothing seems to be happening: refrigeration stopped by the main switch or the thermostat, a defrost in progress, a door open. The EKC 202 and the AK-CC 210 share most codes; the table marks which controller each one applies to.

Which machines use it?

The Danfoss EKC 202 and its successor the AK-CC 210 are panel-mount case controllers used across commercial refrigeration — display cases, cold rooms, and walk-in evaporator control, often tied into a store's front-end monitoring network. Faults show as short codes plus a plain-text line on the display: E-codes are defects (E1 controller fault, E27 and E29 sensor errors) and A-codes are alarms (A1 high temperature, A2 low, A4 door, A45 standby). Practical tip: A45 standby doesn't trip the alarm relay on the AK-CC 210 — a box someone left in standby can sit warming quietly, so treat an unexplained 'Standby mode' display as urgent, not informational.

The Danfoss AK-CC 210 is a case controller for commercial refrigeration — cold rooms, display cases, and walk-in evaporator sections, frequently networked into a central monitoring system that logs its alarms. It reports with the same short-code convention as the rest of Danfoss's EKC/AK line: E-codes for sensor and hardware defects (E25 and E26 are the S3 and S4 air sensors) and A-codes for alarm conditions, including A60, a dedicated HACCP high-temperature alarm for food-safety monitoring. Practical tip: when A60 shows, log the actual product temperature immediately — that alarm is a compliance record as much as an equipment warning, and your notes matter at inspection time.

How do you read and clear the display?

When there's an error the LEDs on the front flash and the alarm relay switches on. Push the top button to see the alarm report, and keep pushing to see any others. E-alarms appear the moment the error occurs; A-alarms only after their time delay has run out, and an A-alarm stays hidden while an E-alarm is active. Push the upper button briefly (1 second) to show the current status code.

What does each code mean — and what should you do?

A1 — High temperature alarm

Applies to: EKC 202 & AK-CC 210

The air temperature has been above the upper alarm limit (parameter A13) for longer than the alarm delay. On the display it reads 'High t. alarm'.

What causes it:

  • Door or lid open or not sealing — a torn gasket loads the box with warm, humid air
  • Warm product just loaded, or a defrost just ended — recovery takes time
  • Ice on the evaporator or a dirty, blocked condenser
  • Condensing unit not running — tripped breaker

Can staff fix it? Often, yes. Doors, gaskets, recent loading, post-defrost recovery, condenser blockage, and breakers are all staff checks. Give it up to an hour to pull back down after fixing the obvious.

What to check, in order:

  1. Check the door or lid is closed and sealing; look for torn gaskets
  2. Check whether warm product was just loaded or a defrost just ended — recovery takes time
  3. Look for ice on the evaporator and blockage or dirt at the condenser
  4. Confirm the condensing unit is running and its breaker is on
  5. Move perishables if temperature keeps rising
Safety: Food safety: log temperatures during the event.

Why it takes a technician: If temperature doesn't recover within that hour, the problem is in the sealed system or controls — that's gauge work requiring EPA Section 608 certification, and warm product is a countdown clock. Log temperatures for your inspection records while you wait.

When to call: If temperature doesn't come back down within an hour after the obvious checks, call a refrigeration tech.

A2 — Low temperature alarm

Applies to: EKC 202 & AK-CC 210

The air temperature has dropped below the lower alarm limit (parameter A14). Displayed as 'Low t. alarm'. Product in a cooler can freeze if this persists.

What causes it:

  • Product stacked against the air sensor, fooling the reading
  • The compressor running continuously and never cycling off
  • A sensor, relay, or setpoint fault driving the box below the A14 limit

Can staff fix it? Partly. Verify the real temperature with a separate thermometer, clear the sensor, and move freeze-sensitive product away from the discharge air. A unit that won't cycle off is beyond staff.

What to check, in order:

  1. Verify actual temperature with a separate thermometer
  2. Check nothing is stacked against the air sensor
  3. Note if the compressor runs continuously and never cycles off
  4. Move freeze-sensitive product away from the discharge air

Why it takes a technician: Diagnosing whether the sensor, relay, or setpoint is at fault means live electrical testing at the controller — and while it runs cold, product in a cooler is freezing.

When to call: If the unit won't cycle off, call a tech to check the sensor, relay, or setpoints.

Source: Danfoss — Controller for temperature control EKC 202A, B, C — user guide (RS8DZ902 (BC162786425567en-000901))

A4 — Door alarm

Applies to: EKC 202 & AK-CC 210

The door contact reports the door has been open longer than the door-alarm delay (parameter A04). Depending on setup, refrigeration and fans may stop while the door is open.

What causes it:

  • Door open longer than the alarm delay — open-door time loads the box with warm, humid air
  • Gasket damage, ice, or product keeping the door off its seal
  • A sticking or iced door switch

Can staff fix it? Usually, yes. Close and latch the door, check the gasket, free the switch. Depending on setup, refrigeration and fans stop while the controller thinks the door is open — so a stuck switch means no cooling.

What to check, in order:

  1. Close the door and confirm it latches
  2. Inspect the gasket for damage, ice, or product blocking the seal
  3. Check the door switch for sticking or ice
  4. Confirm the alarm clears with the door shut

Why it takes a technician: If the alarm won't clear with the door shut, the door switch or its wiring is faulty — live testing of the switch circuit and the A04 delay setting is controller work.

When to call: If the alarm won't clear with the door shut, the door switch or its wiring needs a tech.

Source: Danfoss — Controller for temperature control EKC 202A, B, C — user guide (RS8DZ902 (BC162786425567en-000901))

A45 — Standby mode — refrigeration stopped

Applies to: EKC 202 & AK-CC 210

Refrigeration has been stopped via the r12 main switch or a DI input. The controller shows 'Standby mode'; on the AK-CC 210 the alarm relay is not activated for this state.

What causes it:

  • Refrigeration stopped deliberately via the r12 main switch
  • A DI input (an external switch) put the controller in standby — note the AK-CC 210 does not activate its alarm relay for this state, so nothing else may be flagging it

Can staff fix it? Yes, if someone put it in standby for cleaning or stocking — switch it back and verify box temperature. If nobody did, stop guessing at parameters.

What to check, in order:

  1. Ask whether someone put the unit in standby for cleaning or stocking
  2. Check any external switch wired to the unit
  3. Verify box temperature and move product if it has been off a while

Why it takes a technician: Checking the r12 parameter and DI wiring means going into the controller's setup — a wrong change there keeps the box off. Meanwhile a unit in unnoticed standby is warming product with no alarm relay to warn anyone.

When to call: If no one turned it off, have a tech check the r12 parameter and DI wiring.

Source: Danfoss — Controller for temperature control AK-CC 210 — user guide (RS8EP602, 2018-11 (BC029186426773en-000601))

A59 — Case cleaning

Applies to: EKC 202 & AK-CC 210

A cleaning signal from a DI input has put the case in cleaning mode. On networked systems this event is transmitted and can be logged as documentation of the cleaning sequence.

What causes it:

  • Staff activated cleaning mode via the DI input — a deliberate signal, not a fault
  • The cleaning switch left in the cleaning position after the job was done

Can staff fix it? Yes. Confirm someone actually started a cleaning, return the switch to normal when finished, and verify the case pulls back down to temperature. On networked systems the event is logged as documentation of the cleaning.

What to check, in order:

  1. Confirm whether staff activated cleaning mode
  2. When cleaning is done, return the cleaning switch to normal
  3. Verify the case pulls back down to temperature afterward

Why it takes a technician: If the unit stays in cleaning mode after the switch is returned to normal, the DI input or its configuration is faulty — controller-level diagnosis, and the case isn't refrigerating properly until it's resolved.

When to call: If the unit stays in cleaning mode after the switch is returned to normal, call a tech.

Source: Danfoss — Controller for temperature control EKC 202A, B, C — user guide (RS8DZ902 (BC162786425567en-000901))

E1 — Controller fault

Applies to: EKC 202 & AK-CC 210

Internal fault in the controller itself ('EKC error'). This is a hardware/electronics problem, not a temperature condition.

What causes it:

  • An internal fault in the controller's own electronics — the 'EKC error'
  • Not a temperature condition: the hardware itself is the problem

Can staff fix it? One shot: power-cycle at the breaker for a few minutes. If E1 returns, there's nothing else staff can do except record it and watch box temperature with a separate thermometer.

What to check, in order:

  1. Power-cycle the unit at the breaker for 2-3 minutes
  2. If the code returns, record it and monitor box temperature with a separate thermometer
  3. Move product if the unit is not holding temperature

Why it takes a technician: A persistent internal fault means the controller likely needs replacement — swapping and reprogramming a refrigeration controller is live electrical work, and until then the box's control can't be trusted.

When to call: If E1 persists after a power cycle, the controller likely needs replacement by a tech.

Source: Danfoss — Controller for temperature control EKC 202A, B, C — user guide (RS8DZ902 (BC162786425567en-000901))

E27 — S5 sensor error

Applies to: EKC 202 & AK-CC 210

Error on the S5 sensor — typically the defrost/evaporator sensor (or condenser sensor if configured that way). Defrost control may fall back to time-based operation, risking coil ice-up.

What causes it:

  • The S5 sensor (defrost/evaporator, or condenser if configured that way) has failed
  • With it down, defrost control falls back to time-based operation — coil ice-up is the risk, and heavy ice on the evaporator is the visible symptom

Can staff fix it? Only the checks: look for coil ice, power-cycle once, and monitor box temperature while waiting. The sensor itself is a service part.

What to check, in order:

  1. Check the evaporator coil for heavy ice
  2. Power-cycle at the breaker and see if the code returns
  3. Monitor box temperature while waiting for service

Why it takes a technician: Testing and replacing the S5 sensor is live electrical work at the controller, and an iced coil left unmanaged will eventually block airflow and stop the box from holding temperature.

When to call: If the code persists, a tech needs to test or replace the S5 sensor.

Source: Danfoss — Controller for temperature control EKC 202A, B, C — user guide (RS8DZ902 (BC162786425567en-000901))

E29 — Air sensor (Sair) error

Applies to: EKC 202

Error on the Sair air temperature sensor. The controller can't read box air temperature, so regulation and temperature alarms are unreliable.

What causes it:

  • The Sair air temperature sensor has failed
  • A damaged or iced probe in the return air stream
  • A broken connection between probe and controller

Can staff fix it? Barely. Power-cycle once and inspect the probe for visible damage. If E29 returns, regulation and temperature alarms are unreliable until a tech replaces the sensor — the box is running blind.

What to check, in order:

  1. Check box temperature with a separate thermometer and note it
  2. Look for a damaged or iced sensor probe in the return air stream
  3. Power-cycle at the breaker; if the code returns, plan for service
  4. Move product if temperature drifts out of safe range
Safety: The box is running blind on air temperature — monitor product closely.

Why it takes a technician: The sensor circuit needs live electrical testing, and with no trustworthy air reading, neither control nor the alarms protecting your product can be relied on — monitor with a separate thermometer and move product if it drifts.

When to call: Persistent E29 means the air sensor or wiring must be tested and replaced by a tech.

Source: Danfoss — Controller for temperature control EKC 202A, B, C — user guide (RS8DZ902 (BC162786425567en-000901))

E25 — S3 sensor error

Applies to: AK-CC 210

Error on the S3 sensor (one of the two air sensors, typically mounted before the evaporator). Temperature regulation that uses S3 becomes unreliable.

What causes it:

  • The S3 air sensor (typically mounted before the evaporator) has failed
  • A lost connection between the sensor and the controller
  • Any regulation that uses S3 is unreliable while the fault is active

Can staff fix it? Staff can verify box temperature with a separate thermometer, power-cycle once at the breaker, and record the code and time for the tech. That's the limit.

What to check, in order:

  1. Verify box temperature with a separate thermometer
  2. Power-cycle the unit at the breaker and see if the code returns
  3. Note the code and time for the service tech

Why it takes a technician: Confirming whether the sensor or its wiring failed takes live electrical measurement at the controller — and until it's fixed, temperature control on that sensor can't be trusted.

When to call: If E25 persists, a tech needs to test or replace the S3 sensor.

Source: Danfoss — Controller for temperature control AK-CC 210 — user guide (RS8EP602, 2018-11 (BC029186426773en-000601))

E26 — S4 sensor error

Applies to: AK-CC 210

Error on the S4 sensor (one of the two air sensors, typically mounted after the evaporator). Regulation and alarm functions that use S4 are affected.

What causes it:

  • The S4 air sensor (typically mounted after the evaporator) has failed
  • A lost connection between the sensor and the controller
  • Regulation and alarm functions using S4 are affected while it's down

Can staff fix it? Same as any failed sensor: verify the real temperature independently, power-cycle once, note the code for the service visit. Replacement is not a staff job.

What to check, in order:

  1. Verify box temperature with a separate thermometer
  2. Power-cycle the unit at the breaker and see if the code returns
  3. Note the code and time for the service tech

Why it takes a technician: Sensor and wiring testing is live electrical work, and with S4 out, some of the controller's alarm functions are blind — problems can develop without warning until it's repaired.

When to call: If E26 persists, a tech needs to test or replace the S4 sensor.

Source: Danfoss — Controller for temperature control AK-CC 210 — user guide (RS8EP602, 2018-11 (BC029186426773en-000601))

A16 — Alarm signal from DI2 input

Applies to: AK-CC 210

An external device wired to the DI2 digital input is signaling an alarm after the configured delay. What tripped depends on what is connected to DI2.

What causes it:

  • An external device wired to the DI2 digital input tripped and held past the configured delay
  • Often a protective switch that opened for a reason — what it means depends on what the installer wired to DI2

Can staff fix it? No. Check the box is still cooling, note any other alarms on nearby equipment, and record the code and time — that's real help for the tech, not a fix.

What to check, in order:

  1. Check box temperature and whether the unit still cools
  2. Note any other alarms on nearby equipment
  3. Record the code and time for the tech

Why it takes a technician: Only someone who can trace the installation can identify the device on DI2, and if it's a pressure or safety switch, the root cause behind the trip needs professional diagnosis before the unit is trusted again.

When to call: Call your tech to identify the device on DI2 — it is often a protective switch that tripped for a reason.

A60 — HACCP high-temperature alarm

Applies to: AK-CC 210

High-temperature alarm for the HACCP monitoring function — the food-safety temperature limit tracked by the controller has been exceeded.

What causes it:

  • Temperature exceeded the food-safety limit the controller's HACCP function tracks
  • The same drivers as any high-temperature event: door and gasket leaks letting humid air in, recent loading, evaporator ice, or a dirty condenser that can't reject heat

Can staff fix it? The response is staff work even when the cause isn't: log the actual product and box temperature immediately, check door, loading, coil ice, and condenser airflow, and follow your food-safety procedure for time/temperature abuse — move or discard product per policy.

What to check, in order:

  1. Check and log the actual product/box temperature immediately
  2. Check door seal, recent loading, evaporator ice, and condenser airflow
  3. Follow your food-safety procedure for time/temperature abuse — move or discard product per policy
Safety: Food safety event — document it for HACCP records.

Why it takes a technician: If temperature doesn't recover quickly, the mechanical cause needs a certified tech, and the event itself must be documented for HACCP records — this alarm is as much a compliance event as an equipment one.

When to call: If temperature doesn't recover quickly, call a tech and document the event for HACCP records.

How do you diagnose a Danfoss EKC 202 / AK-CC 210 alarm, step by step?

  1. Read every report. Push the top button to step through the alarm report. Fix E-codes first — an active E-alarm hides any A-alarm behind it.
  2. If it's warm but not alarming, read the status code. A brief press of the upper button shows it: S10 means refrigeration was stopped by the main switch, S11 by the thermostat, S16 by an open DI input, S17 a door open; S14 and S15 are a defrost in progress.
  3. A45 standby is urgent on an AK-CC 210. Refrigeration has been stopped via the r12 main switch or a DI input — and on the AK-CC 210 the alarm relay is not activated, so a remote alarm system won't call anyone.
  4. Know which sensor is which. The EKC 202 uses an air sensor (Sair) and an evaporator/defrost sensor (S5). The AK-CC 210 uses S3 in the air flow before the evaporator, S4 after the evaporator, and S5 on the evaporator for defrost. Live readings are in the service menu (EKC 202: u09 = S5, u69 = Sair).
  5. Replacement sensors must match. Pt1000 sensors are normal; PTC 1000 Ω or NTC 5,000 Ω (at 25 °C) sensors can be used instead, selected in parameter o06 — and all sensors on one controller must be the same type.
  6. A60 is a food-safety record. It's the AK-CC 210's HACCP high-temperature alarm — log the actual product temperature when you see it.
  7. E6 is the clock. Check the battery and reset the clock.
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.
