Danfoss AK-CC 210/250 · E26

What does E26 mean on a Danfoss AK-CC 210/250 walk-in cooler?

Short answer: S4 sensor error. If your Danfoss AK-CC 210/250 is showing E26, the controller has logged a specific, documented condition. This page covers what it means, what causes it, what you can safely fix yourself, and when — and why — it takes a technician.

What exactly is the machine telling you?

Error on the S4 sensor (one of the two air sensors, typically mounted after the evaporator). Regulation and alarm functions that use S4 are affected.

Which machines show this?

The Danfoss AK-CC 210 and AK-CC 250 are case controllers for commercial refrigeration — cold rooms, display cases, and walk-in evaporator sections, frequently networked into a central monitoring system that logs their alarms. They report with the same short-code convention as the rest of Danfoss's EKC/AK line: E-codes for sensor and hardware defects (E25 and E26 are the S3 and S4 air sensors) and A-codes for alarm conditions, including A60, a dedicated HACCP high-temperature alarm for food-safety monitoring. Practical tip: when A60 shows, log the actual product temperature immediately — that alarm is a compliance record as much as an equipment warning, and your notes matter at inspection time.

What causes it?

  • The S4 air sensor (typically mounted after the evaporator) has failed
  • A lost connection between the sensor and the controller
  • Regulation and alarm functions using S4 are affected while it's down

Can you fix it yourself?

Same as any failed sensor: verify the real temperature independently, power-cycle once, note the code for the service visit. Replacement is not a staff job.

Step by step — what to try, in order:

  1. Verify box temperature with a separate thermometer
  2. Power-cycle the unit at the breaker and see if the code returns
  3. Note the code and time for the service tech
One reset is diagnosis, repeated resets are damage. If the same fault returns after a single reset, the underlying condition is real — stop resetting and deal with the cause.

When do you need a technician — and why?

Sensor and wiring testing is live electrical work, and with S4 out, some of the controller's alarm functions are blind — problems can develop without warning until it's repaired.

When to call: If E26 persists, a tech needs to test or replace the S4 sensor.
