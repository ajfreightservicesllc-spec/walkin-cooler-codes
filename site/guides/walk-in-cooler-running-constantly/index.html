Why does my walk-in cooler run constantly and never shut off?

A healthy walk-in cycles: the compressor runs, the box reaches setpoint, the compressor rests. One that runs around the clock is telling you one of three stories — it's fighting more heat than it should, it's too weak to win, or it's being lied to. Each story has its own checks, and running constantly is expensive in every version: electricity burned around the clock, and compressor life consumed at double speed.

Story one: the box is gaining heat it shouldn't

The most common and cheapest-to-fix version. Heat sneaks in through:

  • Door problems — a torn or hardened gasket, a door that doesn't self-close, strip curtains tied back, or heavy traffic propping it open through every delivery. A walk-in door leaking air makes the compressor run to cool the whole kitchen.
  • Infiltration you can see: ice or condensation around the door frame is humid outside air writing you a note.
  • Warm product loading — cooling a rack of hot prep down in the walk-in forces hours of extra runtime. Pre-chill what you can.
  • Panel damage or failed insulation — wet, sagging, or damaged panels lose R-value permanently.

Fix the door and the habits first; they're free-to-cheap and they're the answer a surprising share of the time.

Story two: the system is too weak to reach setpoint

The box gains normal heat, but the refrigeration can't overcome it:

  • An iced evaporator coil blocks airflow — the system runs but moves no cold air. Ice always has a cause; see the ice buildup guide.
  • A filthy condenser can't reject heat — the classic summer version, and the one behind high-condenser alarms like Dixell's HA2, Danfoss's A61/A80, and Carel's cht/CHt codes. Cleaning the coil is legitimate in-house work.
  • Failed evaporator or condenser fans — cold made but not moved.
  • Low refrigerant charge — the system runs endlessly and never quite gets there. That diagnosis needs gauges and an EPA-certified tech, and it means there's a leak to find, not just gas to add.

Story three: the controller is being lied to

A temperature sensor that reads warm keeps calling for cooling forever — the box may actually be at 30°F and freezing product while the display insists it's 42°F. The tell: your independent thermometer disagrees with the display, or product near the evaporator starts freezing. Sensor failures are exactly what codes like Dixell P1, Danfoss E29, and Carel E0 announce — but a drifting sensor can lie without tripping any code, which is why the independent thermometer is the referee.

The order of operations

  1. Verify actual box temperature with an independent thermometer. At setpoint but still running? Suspect the sensor or settings. Above setpoint? Continue.
  2. Inspect the door, gaskets, and closing hardware. Fix what you find.
  3. Look at the evaporator (ice? fans running?) and the condenser (clean? fan running? airflow clear?). Clean the condenser — power off first.
  4. Ask what changed: warm loads, new door traffic, a heat wave, recent service.
  5. Still running constantly after all four? What remains — charge, valves, compressor wear — is instrument territory. In our test markets, the verified contractor lists for Nashville and Birmingham are the shortcut.

One honest warning: constant running is also how walk-ins die young. A compressor designed to cycle that instead runs 24/7 is aging on fast-forward — the electric bill is the visible cost, and the shortened compressor life is the bigger invisible one. This symptom rewards fixing early.
